About Marit

I’ve spent a decade and a half working the Hubbard County lakes. I stayed because this is a place you only learn by being here: what the ice does in March, which shorelines go soft in a wet spring, which roads hold up once the plow has been through a few times.

The seasons are the actual job. Most lake property out here only looks like itself for four months of the year, so I ask people to see a place twice if they can: once in August, and once when the dock is stacked on the shore. The second visit usually tells you more.

A cabin and a house are different sales, and I won’t run one as the other. A cabin sells on the water, the access and what the road does in winter. A house in town sells on the roof, the furnace and the street. They need different questions, asked at different times of year.

If this is your first place on the water, I’ll say it plainly: the lot, the shoreline and the access are the parts you can’t change later. Septic, well and shoreline questions come up on nearly every lake property, and my job is to get them onto your list early and in front of the right people.

The lake country

Park Rapids sits in the pine and birch of Hubbard County, with lakes on every side of it and four hard seasons to get through. This is what the place is like.

Fishhook Lake in July.

Main Avenue

Downtown Park Rapids runs along a street so wide that cars park down the center of it as well as along both curbs. Summer fills it. In the off-season it goes still, and the storefronts catch the low light from end to end.

Main Avenue at 2nd Street, Park Rapids.

The Heartland State Trail

One of the first rail-to-trail projects in the country sets out from Park Rapids along the old rail bed. It’s paved the whole way through Walker to Cass Lake, and it runs under pine and birch for most of the ride.

The Heartland State Trail leaving Park Rapids.

Itasca State Park

A short drive north, Minnesota’s oldest state park protects the old pine around Lake Itasca and the headwaters of the Mississippi. Go on an off-season morning, when the light comes in low through the trunks.

The Mississippi headwaters at Itasca State Park.

The lakes

Fish Hook, Potato and a great many more, lined with cabins, resorts, seasonal places and year-round homes. The lake economy does most of its living in summer. Winters run deep. In late September the color comes on and the light lies flat across the water.

A lake through the pines at Itasca State Park.

A year on the lakes

What each season does to a sale out here.

Spring

Ice-out starts the year here, and the shoulder season after it is soft in every sense. The roads are soft, the shorelines are wet and the lake is cold and plain. I like showing a place now, because nothing is hiding.

The Fish Hook River in late May.

Summer

The short, loud season, when everything moves at once. The docks are in, the boats are out and every lake place looks its best, which is lovely and not always useful. I like you to have done your thinking before July gets here.

The Park Theater on Main Avenue.

Autumn

This is the low, honest light, when a lake place shows truthfully. The boats come out, the water goes still and you can stand on a dock and get a fair idea of the other ten months. It’s my favorite time to walk a shoreline with someone.

October color at Itasca State Park.

Winter

Winter is when you can finally see the lot, the roofline and what the road is really like. The leaves are down, the dock is stacked on the shore and the place has nothing left to flatter it. If you still want it in February, you want it.

Last light at Itasca State Park.
